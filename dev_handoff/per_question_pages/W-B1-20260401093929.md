## Development Markers (3 specific + 1 generic 4th)

### 1. When and why you listen to music

- **Score 1 (mentioned):** says they listen to music with no specific time or reason
- **Score 2 (developed):** names at least one specific time/situation AND a specific reason (e.g., I listen on the bus to work because it makes the commute pass faster)
- **Score 3 (extended):** all of 2 AND a NAMED song title (e.g., 'Yesterday'), artist name (Coldplay, Beyoncé), or real genre (gospel, amapiano, hip-hop, classical, rock). Mood-based or function-based categories like 'spiritual songs', 'motivational songs', 'sad songs' do NOT count.

### 2. How music affects your mood

- **Score 1 (mentioned):** states music affects mood with no specific change
- **Score 2 (developed):** describes a specific mood change AND links it to a type of music (e.g., fast songs energise me; slow songs calm me)
- **Score 3 (extended):** all of 2 AND a NAMED song, artist, or specific situation. Categorical descriptions like 'spiritual songs' or 'motivational songs' do NOT satisfy 'named song/artist'.

### 3. Personal example to support argument

- **Score 1 (mentioned):** vague reference to a personal music experience (e.g., I love music)
- **Score 2 (developed):** a specific anecdote with at least one named song, occasion, or feeling
- **Score 3 (extended):** a fully developed anecdote with NAMED music (actual title or artist), specific situation, AND emotional outcome. A description like 'a song I memorised' does NOT count as named music.

### 4. Additional on-topic development

- **Score 1 (mentioned):** introduces a DISTINCT extra on-topic point — not a rephrasing or repeat of any other requirement — within the question's subject, with no further detail
- **Score 2 (developed):** introduces a distinct extra on-topic point AND develops it meaningfully (a real reason, cause, or effect — not just length)
- **Score 3 (extended):** introduces a distinct extra on-topic point AND develops it AND supports it with a concrete specific that genuinely advances the argument
